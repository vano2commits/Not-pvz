Design document · scoped to the first world

Hoard

A lane defence roguelite. You are the goblins, retreating deeper underground with a cart of stolen junk, and the heroes coming to take it back are the horde.

v1 · world one only Godot 2D · prototype in web Run length target: ~20 min
How to read this

built means it exists in the playable prototype and has been tested. proposed means it is designed but unbuilt — treat every one of those as a guess until a playtest says otherwise. cut means deliberately excluded, with the reason recorded so we don't relitigate it monthly.

This covers world one only. Nothing here commits the rest of the game. The document is meant to be edited as playtests contradict it — a design doc that stops changing has stopped being read.

01 The pitch

Plants vs. Zombies' lane defence, with the roles inverted and a fusion engine where the plant roster used to be.

Grubby little goblins defend a cart of worthless treasure — bottle caps, one good boot, a doorknob — against gleaming, self-important adventurers who want it back. Both sides are completely sincere. The goblins genuinely believe the doorknob is priceless, and they may be right: the caravan stole something that matters and nobody knows which thing it is.

The thing that makes it not-a-reskin is fusion. Three goblins are for sale and nothing else; everything past that is made by dropping one goblin onto another until up to three share a body. Your board is never finished, and the answer to a new threat is always another goblin, never a shop.

Validated in prototype

The loop is fun before art: a tester reached wave 25 unprompted and described merging as “the most interactive part.” The design below is built outward from that sentence.

Between chambers

You pick one of two routes down. Each names its hazard before you choose it, so a bad chamber is a decision rather than a dice roll. Hazards change the board itself — cells buried, outer lanes sealed, low ground flooded — and stack for the rest of the run. built

This is also where the story lives. No cutscenes: a line or two of flavour on the route cards, and occasionally a demand from the Reeve. It costs nothing to skip and nothing to produce.

03 The core loop

  1. Coins fall from the heroes you kill. Nothing accrues from the sky and nothing needs collecting — a purse spills where the hero died and walks itself to the cart. built
  2. Place goblins in a 7×5 grid. Coins buy goblins and nothing else. built
  3. Dig for the rest. The rock is visibly richer toward the far end, so a Digger's worth is decided by how far forward you dare stand it, and its shaft deepens the longer it survives. The gradient is continuous — no tiles, no lottery. built
  4. Waves arrive when the board is clear, plus a breather long enough to spend a purse in. built
  5. Fuse to answer what the waves are doing to you. built
  6. Descend, take a hazard, repeat. Something reaches the cart, the run ends.
Lanes open as the level goes on

A chamber does not start on all five lanes. Three are open, the fourth caves in at wave 4 and the fifth at wave 5, centre-out — the same trick 1-1 plays by opening on a single lane. Closed lanes are drawn as rubble and refuse placement, so the rule needs no text. This was the fix for a wave 3 that testers called impossible: a scripted playthrough spent 66% of the level under the price of a Chucker, and of the 74s where a lane stood undefended, 65s were spent unable to afford the answer. The problem was never damage — it was being asked to cover more ground than a cleared wave had paid for. built

Why failure is terminal, not a drain

An earlier build let heroes steal coins gradually. It produced the PvZ2 feel-bad: a slow bleed where the correct play is to write off a lane. Now a breach fires that lane's single bear trap, and with the trap spent the run ends. Binary and dramatic beats gradual and grim. built

04 Fusion — the differentiator

The roster is three goblins. Everything else in the game is something you built by dropping one onto another. A body holds up to three parts in any mix, and what comes out is a named creature with its own job, not a stat line with a bigger number. built

Why it works this way

An earlier version sold dedicated upgrade cards you carried in and planted on top of a goblin, the way a Gatling Pea goes on a Repeater. That trade is tile efficiency, and it only pays in a mode where tiles are scarce — PvZ has Survival for exactly that, and we do not. It also spends a card slot on something that is not a creature. Both the PvZ 1 Fusion mods and PvZ3 do the opposite: you drag a plant onto a plant and get a genuinely new species with combined abilities and its own sprite. The incentive there is capability, not space, and it is the one thing anyone likes about PvZ3. We follow that.

What the parts do

PartsResultWhat changes
ChuckerChuckerLobs a rock at the first hero in the lane.
Chucker ×2Bowpair They share one bow too big for either of them. Harder hit, pierces two.
Chucker ×3Ballista Crew Three goblins crew a ballista. Skewers the whole lane.
Digger ×nDeep Shaft, Delve Each extra Digger raises the take ×1.85 and warms the shaft faster.

Eighteen combinations are named, which is the whole point — a Toll Fort reads as a thing that exists rather than as barricade+chucker+digger. Hybrids pay 0.72× on pooled health, so fusing is not free, and refunds follow what was actually spent, so a fused goblin is worth what went into it.

Still to prove

Three parts to a body is a guess. It is enough for the Chucker ladder to read as a story (one throws, two share a bow, three crew a ballista) and it keeps the recipe count at eighteen rather than in the hundreds. If players top out early and want more, the cap is the first thing to move.

Fusions built

Eighteen results across every mix of up to three parts, each named so it reads as its own creature: Bowpair and Ballista Crew up the Chucker ladder; Deep Shaft and Delve up the Digger one; Bunker, Bulwark, Bastion, Redoubt and Great Wall for armour; and the crossbreeds — Tollkeeper, Strongbox, Mint, Vault, Counting House, Toll Fort, Prospector's Guard.

Junk built

Drops only. Never sold, never required. Six slots on a rank-3 goblin. Dented Helm (+health), Rusty Spring (×1.6 rate), Jar of Bees (three-way shot), Guttering Torch (burn), Bundle of Spikes (thorns). Combinations rename the goblin — a Chucker with bees and a torch is the Wrath of the Doorknob — and that naming is most of the discovery.

07 Unlocks

Variety, never power. A veteran's run is not easier than a beginner's, it has more interesting options in it. This is the deliberate opposite of PvZ2, where progress meant being allowed to win. proposed

08 Story, and how little of it there is

The goblins never speak. Like the plants, they are characterful through design and behaviour only. All narration sits in two cheap places: route cards between chambers, and the Reeve's demands, which arrive as a line of text before the chambers she has reinforced.

The premise resolves the obvious question — why would heroes chase goblins this far? — in one sentence: the caravan stole something that matters, and the goblins do not know which thing it is. That keeps the joke (they are sincere about the doorknob), explains escalating pursuit, and scales past humans: deeper things want it more, and the adventuring party is merely who reached you first.

World one's cast is human authority: squires, knights, a Reeve enforcing a claim. Later worlds are not human, and that shift is the reward for finishing this one.

09 Deliberately not doing

Kept here so we stop reopening them. cut

  • Enemies that bypass the line entirely (the Tunneller). Arriving at chamber 3 it asked players to solve a puzzle before learning the basics. PvZ waits for the pool levels, hours in. Deferred to a later world, not deleted.
  • A shop. Counters are goblins, so junk can stay pure random upside with nothing gated behind a drop.
  • Two resources. Tried and rejected on paper — it usually just means waiting twice.
  • The hoard as a health bar. One number doing currency, income and lose-condition was elegant and did not survive contact with a player; it read as ordinary sun.
  • Permanent stat upgrades. See section 7.
  • Dedicated upgrade cards. A card you carry in only to plant on top of another goblin buys tile efficiency, and tiles are not scarce enough here to pay for it. Fusion uses the goblins already on the board instead. See section 4.
  • Anything mandatory placed by RNG. Twice now a design has put a required resource somewhere random — the Tunneller's counter, then golden tiles — and both times the run was decided before the player touched it. Randomness is allowed to add upside. It is not allowed to own a counter, an economy, or a place to stand.
  • Worlds two onward, boss variants, dialogue, meta-currency. Not until world one is genuinely good.

10 Build order

Ten chambers is not the first milestone. The first milestone is the first ten minutes, because that is what tells you whether any of the remaining work is worth doing.

M1

Chambers 1–3 in Godot, polished

Port the validated loop. Real art for five goblins and three pursuers, sound, the merge feedback. Nothing procedural beyond what exists. Ship this to ten testers.

M2

The full ten-chamber run

Pavise, Bannerman, the hazard chamber, the Reeve. Difficulty tuned against real players rather than a script.

M3

Unlocks and the meta layer

Milestone tracking, the four unlockable goblins, the run-summary screen. Only worth building once people want a second run.

M4

Feel pass

Animation, screen shake, the sound of a merge. This is where a competent game becomes one people talk about, and it cannot be done early.

11 Open questions

These are unresolved and should be resolved by playtest, not by argument.

  • Does anyone fuse across types? The Chucker ladder is legible and rewarding, so the risk is that everyone builds Ballista Crews and never makes a Toll Fort. If the crossbreeds go unused, the fix is to give them a job the pure ladders cannot do, not to nerf the ladder.
  • Is three parts the right cap? It keeps the recipe list at eighteen and gives the Chucker its one-two-three story. Whether it leaves enough room to keep building late is unknown.
  • Is the late game too rich? Scripted runs finish with several hundred coins unspent. Some of that is the script running out of ideas rather than the economy overpaying, but it wants watching on a real playthrough — a fusion you can always afford is not a decision.
  • Is twenty minutes the right run? Untested. Ten chambers might want to be six.
  • Does the invisible tutorial land? If testers never work out the Leaper rule unprompted, the teaching is wrong — add a clearer visual, not a text box.
  • Does a losing run feel worth having played? The single most important question for any roguelite, and the one most easily answered too late.
