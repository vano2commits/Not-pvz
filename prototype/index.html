    What changed, and what it's testing

    The hoard is no longer a health bar. Nothing drains it — if a pursuer reaches the caravan your one bear trap in that lane fires, and if that trap is already spent, the run ends there. A clean terminal fail rather than a slow bleed.

    • Merging costs you power and buys you slots. Two goblins of matching kind and rank merge into a higher rank inheriting both their junk — but output grows sub-linearly (×1.7 per rank, not ×2), so a merge is a deliberate loss of raw damage. What it buys is board space and item slots: 2 at rank 1, 4 at rank 2, 6 at rank 3. Deep fusion is only reachable through rank, and rank caps at 4. Is that a real decision, or still just "merge everything"?
    • Every threat ships with its answer, and the answer is never a dice roll. Two pursuers ignore a static line entirely, and both counters are fusions rather than new cards — but every item is always buyable at the junk stall, so drops are a discount rather than a gate. A new kind of pursuer is announced during the breather before its first appearance, named alongside the thing that stops it. Does the re-solve loop land, or does it read as unfair?
    • Depth is a place, not a multiplier. Every fifth wave you pick a route down. Each chamber changes the board itself — cells buried, lanes sealed, ground flooded — and hazards stack for the rest of the run. Does a visible board change beat a stat penalty you can't see?
    • There is finally something worth saving for. The Ballista costs 350 — seven Chuckers — and pierces a whole lane. Does one genuinely expensive option create a real spend-or-wait decision?

    The pursuers

    Wave construction, spawn pacing, per-enemy speed variance and the bleed-out rule are still taken from the original's decompiled source. Fiction, for anyone reading the numbers: the caravan stole something that matters, and the goblins do not know which thing it is.
