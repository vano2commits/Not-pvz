    What changed, and what it's testing

    The hoard is no longer a health bar. Nothing drains it — if a pursuer reaches the caravan your one bear trap in that lane fires, and if that trap is already spent, the run ends there. A clean terminal fail rather than a slow bleed.

    • Merging goes two ways. Two goblins of the same kind and rank become a higher rank — but output grows sub-linearly (×1.7, not ×2), so ranking up is a deliberate loss of raw damage that buys board space and item slots: 2 at rank 1, 4 at rank 2, 6 at rank 3, capped at rank 4. Two goblins of different kinds instead make a hybrid that holds both jobs at 70% each — a Chucker and a Barricade become a Bunker, a wall that shoots. Ten pairings, each with its own name. Is choosing between depth and breadth a real decision?
    • Goblins are the answer to enemies. Junk is only ever a bonus. Nothing is for sale — junk drops from the dead and that is the only way to get any. So no counter can be gated behind a drop, because counters are not items: a Leaper clears a rank-1 goblin and nothing taller, so merging anything in that lane stops it. Nobody is told this. The Leaper simply bounces off, sits down hard and sees stars, and ranked goblins are visibly bigger. Threats that bypass your line outright are held back for deeper chambers, the way PvZ waits for the pool levels to introduce anything that swims past you. Does the rule teach itself, or does it need saying out loud?
    • Depth is a place, not a multiplier. Every fifth wave you pick a route down. Each chamber changes the board itself — cells buried, lanes sealed, ground flooded — and hazards stack for the rest of the run. Does a visible board change beat a stat penalty you can't see?
    • Coins buy goblins and nothing else. With no shop, every coin is a placement or a merge, and the Ballista at 350 — seven Chuckers — is the only thing worth going without for. Does one genuinely expensive option create a real spend-or-wait decision?

    The pursuers — designer reference; the game never states any of the answers

    Wave construction, spawn pacing, per-enemy speed variance and the bleed-out rule are still taken from the original's decompiled source. Fiction, for anyone reading the numbers: the caravan stole something that matters, and the goblins do not know which thing it is.
